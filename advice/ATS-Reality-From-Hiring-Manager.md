# How ATSs Actually Work: Insights from an Engineering Hiring Manager

## Background

I've been a hiring manager for 3 different defense/aerospace companies, using two different ATSs (Workday and Greenhouse). Currently hiring for 6 positions (3 entry level, 3 mid career) at a prestigious aerospace company. In the last month alone, I've reviewed 136 applications for these positions.

*Note: This perspective is specific to defense/aerospace and may differ from full software companies.*

## The Reality About ATSs

### ATSs Don't Auto-Reject Resumes

**Myth**: ATSs automatically filter out resumes based on keywords.

**Reality**: ATSs are primarily tracking systems for managing applicants through the hiring process. The only automatic rejections occur when hiring managers set up "must-have" requirements with specific screening questions:

- "Do you have a Secret clearance?"
- "Have you been in your current position for at least 12 months?"

Answering "no" to these critical requirements results in automatic rejection.

### Recruiters vs. Hiring Managers

**Reality**: Recruiters often lack technical expertise to pre-screen technical resumes effectively. For engineering positions, recruiters typically don't have technical degrees, so ALL resumes are pushed directly to the hiring manager (or designated technical screener).

I personally read every single resume that comes through.

## What Really Irritates Hiring Managers

### 1. Unqualified Applications
Applying for jobs you don't meet basic qualifications for. If I'm hiring engineers and you have a political science degree, why would I choose you over 130+ qualified engineering candidates?

### 2. Poor Resume Formatting
- **Two-column resumes** (especially with photos) - This screams "trying to fill space"
- **Including photos** - Unprofessional and potentially problematic

### 3. Generic, Untailored Resumes
- Objective sections mentioning other industries (oil & gas, metallurgy) when applying for manufacturing roles
- No connection between your experience and the specific job requirements
- Generic language that doesn't speak to the role

## The Hiring Process Reality

### 4. Time Investment
The process takes significant time. I review resumes daily and make immediate decisions (reject or advance), but this is atypical. Most hiring managers need time to:
- Screen all candidates
- Set up interviews  
- Balance hiring duties with their primary job responsibilities

## Keywords vs. Buzzwords

### 5. Strategic Keyword Usage
- **Buzzwords**: Detrimental and obvious
- **Relevant keywords**: Essential for quick qualification assessment

If I'm seeking MRB (Material Review Board) experience, I want to see:
- "preliminary review"
- "material review" 
- "MRB" itself

As a hiring manager, I need to quickly determine qualifications without reading between the lines or making assumptions.

## Credibility Matters

### 6. BS Detection
I'm an expert in my field and can immediately spot inflated claims. Padding your resume with unrealistic achievements (like "saved $2 million as an intern") is a red flag. If you make it to an interview, expect to be grilled on these claims.

## Application Patterns

### 7. Multiple Applications Visibility
Yes, I can see all positions you've applied for within the company. This matters because:

**Red flag**: 39 applications across completely different functions suggests lack of focus and makes it easy to reject you for better-aligned candidates.

**Positive signal**: 5 applications all within the same specialty (e.g., design) shows clear career direction and urgency to secure you before competitors do.

## Key Takeaways

1. **ATSs are tracking tools, not filtering tools** (except for hard requirements)
2. **Hiring managers read your resume, not algorithms**
3. **Tailor every application** to the specific role and company
4. **Be honest about achievements** - inflated claims will be challenged
5. **Focus your applications** on roles that truly match your background
6. **Use relevant keywords** naturally, not as buzzword stuffing
7. **Professional formatting matters** - avoid gimmicks and photos

The hiring process is human-driven, not algorithm-driven. Your resume needs to quickly communicate relevant qualifications to a busy hiring manager who may be reviewing 100+ applications for a single position.

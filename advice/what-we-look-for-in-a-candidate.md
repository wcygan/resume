# What We Look for in a Candidate

*Based on [Chip Huyen's article](https://huyenchip.com/2023/01/24/what-we-look-for-in-a-candidate.html)*

## Key Hiring Criteria

### 1. Demonstrated Expertise
- **Show, don't just tell**: Don't just list skills - demonstrate how you've actually used them
- **Provide context**: Explain your technical contributions and their significance
- **Share public evidence**: Include links to open source contributions, technical blog posts, or other visible work
- **Quality over quantity**: Deep expertise in a few areas is better than shallow knowledge across many

### 2. Getting Things Done
- **Show initiative**: Highlight projects you've started, not just participated in
- **Demonstrate persistence**: Show consistent contributions and ability to drive work to completion
- **Prove commitment**: Employers are wary of frequent job changes - show stability and growth
- **Lead by example**: Include instances where you've taken ownership or leadership

### 3. Unique Perspectives
- **Avoid cookie-cutter projects**: Showcase distinctive work that stands out from typical bootcamp or tutorial projects
- **Highlight unconventional approaches**: Show creative problem-solving and original thinking
- **Demonstrate diversity of experience**: Different backgrounds can bring valuable perspectives
- **Show personality**: Let your unique voice and interests come through

### 4. Impact Over Metrics
- **Quantify meaningfully**: Connect numbers to actual business value, not just vanity metrics
- **Explain your role**: Be specific about your individual contributions to team achievements
- **Include qualitative achievements**: Mentorship, internal recognition, process improvements
- **Business context matters**: Show understanding of how your work affects the larger organization

## Resume Best Practices

### Format and Structure
- **Keep it concise**: Preferably one page, maximum two
- **Use PDF format**: Ensures consistent formatting across devices
- **Include meaningful links**: GitHub, portfolio, blog posts, or other relevant public work
- **Remove redundancy**: Eliminate repetitive or irrelevant information

### Content Strategy
- **Customize for company size**: Smaller companies may value different experiences than large corporations
- **Focus on recent and relevant**: Prioritize experiences most applicable to the target role
- **Use active language**: Start bullet points with strong action verbs
- **Quantify when possible**: But ensure metrics are meaningful and contextual

### Cover Letters
- **Keep it brief**: A short, focused cover letter can help explain your fit
- **Customize for each role**: Generic cover letters are easily spotted
- **Address gaps or transitions**: Use it to explain career changes or unusual paths
- **Show research**: Demonstrate knowledge of the company and role

## What Employers Really Want

### Technical Skills
- **Depth over breadth**: Better to be excellent at a few technologies than mediocre at many
- **Current and relevant**: Stay updated with industry trends and technologies
- **Applied knowledge**: Show how you've used skills in real-world scenarios
- **Continuous learning**: Demonstrate growth and adaptation over time

### Soft Skills
- **Communication**: Ability to explain complex technical concepts clearly
- **Collaboration**: Evidence of successful teamwork and cross-functional work
- **Problem-solving**: Examples of tackling ambiguous or challenging problems
- **Adaptability**: Flexibility in changing environments or requirements

### Cultural Fit
- **Alignment with values**: Research company culture and demonstrate compatibility
- **Growth mindset**: Show willingness to learn and take on new challenges
- **Initiative**: Examples of going beyond assigned responsibilities
- **Reliability**: Consistent delivery and dependable work ethic

## Red Flags to Avoid

- Frequent job changes without clear progression or learning
- Lack of concrete examples or details about contributions
- Generic applications that could apply to any company
- Overemphasis on credentials without demonstrating practical application
- Poor communication or presentation of information
- Unrealistic salary expectations or demands
- Negative comments about previous employers or colleagues

## Key Takeaways

1. **Be specific and concrete** in describing your experiences and contributions
2. **Show progression and growth** throughout your career
3. **Demonstrate impact** through both quantitative and qualitative measures
4. **Highlight unique value** you bring beyond technical skills
5. **Research and customize** your application for each opportunity
6. **Keep learning and growing** - show continuous improvement and adaptation

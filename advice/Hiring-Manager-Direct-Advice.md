# Direct Advice from a Hiring Manager (12 YoE)

*Source: Meta hiring manager with ~5 years of hiring experience, processing ~350 resumes per week*

## Key Context

- Hiring managers have full-time jobs beyond reviewing resumes
- Internal recruiters juggle multiple positions simultaneously
- Information must be quickly digestible - any difficulty leads to immediate rejection
- Manual rejection is the bigger hurdle than AI filters in ATS systems

## Resume Guidelines

### Critical Formatting Rules

**❌ Avoid These Common Mistakes:**
- **2-page resumes** - NO reason it can't fit on 1 page
- **Double spacing** - Makes resumes impossible to read quickly and confuses ATS parsing
- **3+ line bullet points** - Shows you don't understand bullet points
- **Professional summaries** - Skip AI-generated paragraphs, reclaim the space
- **Oversized margins** - Use standard or smaller margins
- **Massive font sizes** - Name can be larger but not 72pt
- **Broken URLs** - Check all LinkedIn/GitHub links
- **Inconsistent fonts** - Different point sizes across sections look unprofessional

**✅ Do This Instead:**
- **Single page maximum** - Drop old/irrelevant roles if needed
- **Single spacing** - Clean, readable format
- **1-2 line bullets** - Concise, impactful points
- **Standard margins** - Maximize content space
- **Consistent formatting** - Same fonts and sizes throughout
- **Verified links** - All URLs should work

### Content Guidelines

**Skills Section:**
- Keep to 1-2 lines as highlights, or omit entirely
- Don't list skills you've never actually used
- Don't include irrelevant skills (Word, Photoshop, etc.)
- Don't vertically list skills - wastes valuable space

**Education:**
- Format: University - Degree, Major
- Year is optional
- Include both bachelor's and master's if applicable

**Experience:**
- Recent roles only - too many recent jobs is a red flag
- Focus on relevant accomplishments
- Use action-oriented language

### Legal/Compliance Notes

- Be honest about visa sponsorship needs
- Lying about sponsorship status = automatic disqualification
- Dishonesty is discriminated against, not origin

### AI Usage Warning

- **Don't AI-generate your resume** - Everyone can tell, it's an auto-reject
- **Don't AI-generate cover letters** - Use them for short, specific role highlights

## Interview Advice

### Take-Home Assignments

**Do:**
- Solve the problem as given
- Write clean, readable code
- Keep it simple - don't overthink

**Don't:**
- Add excessive comments or verbose syntax
- Use AI to solve problems - companies know the AI responses
- Overcomplicate simple problems

### Live Interviews

**Treat as Conversation:**
- Stay casual and conversational
- Handle stress professionally (it's part of the test)
- Take time to think before speaking

**Technical Problem Solving:**
- Actually solve the assigned problem
- Don't get stuck on sub-problems
- Don't over-optimize details
- Talk through your approach, then write code

**Behavioral Guidelines:**
- Don't use AI during interviews (it's obvious)
- Admit knowledge gaps honestly - don't BS
- Don't lie about experience - interviewers test claimed knowledge
- Don't ramble for 5-10 minutes without structure

## Key Takeaways

1. **Readability is everything** - Hiring managers need to grok information quickly
2. **Format matters more than you think** - 70% of resumes have formatting issues
3. **Honesty over cleverness** - Lies and exaggerations are easily caught
4. **Solve the actual problem** - Don't get distracted by edge cases or optimizations
5. **One page, single spaced, clean format** - This alone puts you ahead of most candidates

## Bottom Line

The goal is to make the hiring manager's job easier, not harder. Every friction point in reading your resume or interviewing with you increases the likelihood of rejection. Focus on clear communication, honest representation, and solving the problems you're actually given.

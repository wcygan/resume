# Interview preparation

Generate questions that help the candidate defend actual resume claims. This
is rehearsal, not prediction of an exact interview loop.

## Question construction

Every question must cite:

- the resume claim it probes;
- the supporting work-experience entry to review; and
- the reason the claim deserves scrutiny for the target role.

Probe five dimensions as applicable:

1. **Mechanism:** How did the system or change work?
2. **Ownership:** What did the candidate decide and deliver versus the team?
3. **Outcome:** How was the number measured, over what period, and against what
   baseline?
4. **Tradeoff:** Which alternatives were rejected, and why?
5. **Failure and learning:** What went wrong, what remained risky, and what
   would change next time?

## Priority

Prioritize claims with large outcomes, high scale, strong ownership language,
cross-team influence, or complex reliability properties. Use the relevance
score as the author's prior, then verify that the resume and source actually
support the claim.

Difficulty labels may describe rehearsal depth:

- **Foundation:** establish context and role.
- **Mechanism:** explain the design and implementation.
- **Tradeoff:** defend judgment under alternatives and constraints.
- **Adversarial:** resolve ambiguous attribution, causality, or an apparently
  inflated number.

Do not call a question a likely interview question without external evidence.

## Output

For each question include:

```text
Question:
Resume claim:
Source to review:
Why it matters:
Strong answer should establish:
Evidence still needed:
```

Finish with the five highest-value drills and a coverage list of consequential
claims that were not exercised. A coverage gap means the rehearsal is
incomplete; it does not predict interview risk.

# Content review

Review the resume through a small set of analytical lenses. These are
questions to apply, not personas to simulate and not independent votes.

## Lenses

### Fast-scan clarity

- Can a reader identify role, company, scope, and strongest outcomes quickly?
- Does each bullet reveal its useful distinction before secondary mechanics?
- Are repeated phrases or overly broad technology inventories consuming space?

### Claim support and defensibility

- Does the source support the number, causal language, status, and ownership?
- Could the candidate explain how the result was measured and what they did?
- Does wording imply sole ownership where the source describes shared work?

### Technical mechanism depth

- Does a technically important bullet name the mechanism that made the outcome
  possible?
- Is the mechanism differentiating, or merely a technology list?
- Are tradeoffs or reliability properties visible when they establish senior
  judgment?

### Scope and level signal

- Does the resume show ownership across systems, teams, migrations, or durable
  platform adoption rather than only task execution?
- Are leadership claims supported by decisions, coordination, enablement, or
  sustained ownership?
- Does the document show progression without inflating titles?

### Narrative and target fit

- Do profile, experience, projects, and skills reinforce the same intended
  backend, platform, infrastructure, or product-engineering narrative?
- Are high-priority experiences easy to find for the supplied role?
- Is content omitted because it is irrelevant, not merely because it is hard
  to summarize?

### Visual hierarchy and density

Apply this lens only after inspecting the actual PDF. Check page balance,
wrapping, typography, hierarchy, clipping, and whether a long bullet earns its
space. Source line count is not rendered evidence.

## Prioritization

Rank findings by:

1. unsupported or misleading claims;
2. missing high-value evidence for the target role;
3. obscured ownership, mechanism, or outcome;
4. narrative or ordering problems;
5. compression and polish.

Return at most eight findings unless the user requests an exhaustive audit.
Do not create a numeric score unless the user supplies a reviewed rubric with
a meaningful denominator.

## Output

Use a compact overview followed by prioritized findings. For each finding use
the evidence-led fields from `01-evidence-model.md`. Finish with:

- what should remain unchanged;
- the highest-value next edit;
- checks needed if the edit is implemented; and
- unresolved evidence questions.
